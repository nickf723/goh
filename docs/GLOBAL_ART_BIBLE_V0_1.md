# Grace of Humanity — Global Art Bible v0.1

## Purpose

This document defines the global visual grammar for *Grace of Humanity*. It is intentionally broader than any single biome, dungeon, element, culture, era, or concept image.

A location should feel unmistakably like *Grace of Humanity* even when its palette, architecture, technology, climate, and mood change completely.

The Green Earth shrine study is the first successful test case, not the template for every environment.

---

## Global Visual Thesis

**Stylized fantasy realism built from clear expressive forms, saturated but grounded color, believable materials and lighting, monumental environments, and tightly controlled detail hierarchy.**

The world should combine:

- immediate visual readability;
- expressive simplification;
- rich but disciplined color;
- physical weight and believable material response;
- ancient, mythic, or technological grandeur;
- painterly composition rather than indiscriminate realism.

A useful reference axis is:

```text
stylized adventure-fantasy readability
                ↕
monumental grounded fantasy weight
```

The target lives between those poles rather than copying either one directly.

---

# 1. Art-Direction Hierarchy

Every visual decision belongs to a layer. Lower layers may vary dramatically, but should inherit the rules above them.

```text
GLOBAL ART BIBLE
    ↓
ELEMENTAL / THEMATIC DIALECT
    ↓
REGIONAL / CULTURAL DIALECT
    ↓
HISTORICAL / TECHNOLOGICAL DIALECT
    ↓
LOCATION ART BRIEF
    ↓
ASSET / SHOT / ROOM EXECUTION
```

### Global Art Bible
Defines what makes every scene belong to the same game.

### Elemental / Thematic Dialect
Earth, Fire, Dreams, Death, Space, Time, etc. influence shape, motion, material, light, effects, and environmental behavior.

### Regional / Cultural Dialect
Architecture, ornament, ecology, craft traditions, settlement patterns, and local materials respond to place and culture.

### Historical / Technological Dialect
Ancient ruins, 19th-century machinery, Renaissance workshops, angelic infrastructure, modern technology, and alien construction each alter the visual vocabulary.

### Location Art Brief
Defines the specific visual thesis of one dungeon, settlement, room, vista, or encounter.

### Asset Execution
Individual models, textures, VFX, lighting setups, props, and animations serve the higher-level composition.

No lower layer may override global readability merely to become more detailed or spectacular.

---

# 2. Non-Negotiable Global Pillars

## 2.1 Large Forms Before Detail

Every environment, structure, creature, machine, and prop must work first as a silhouette or blockout.

The production order is:

```text
large form
→ medium form
→ focal detail
→ surface breakup
→ atmosphere / VFX
```

If a scene is confusing as gray boxes, more decoration is not the solution.

---

## 2.2 Readability Is Sacred

The player should quickly understand:

- where Grace is;
- where she can move;
- what is dangerous;
- what is interactive;
- what is important;
- where the scene wants the eye to travel.

Important subjects must separate through one or more of:

- silhouette;
- value;
- hue;
- lighting;
- motion;
- scale;
- negative space.

Visual complexity must never erase navigation.

---

## 2.3 Stylized Form, Grounded Rendering

Geometry may be simplified, exaggerated, painterly, or iconic.

Rendering should still give it physical credibility through:

- believable light falloff;
- readable roughness differences;
- coherent wetness and reflectivity;
- contact shadow and grounding;
- atmospheric depth;
- material-specific response.

The goal is not photorealism. The goal is **convincing physical presence**.

---

## 2.4 Saturated, Not Candy-Colored

The game should be more colorful than grim realism while retaining believable relationships between materials and lighting.

Saturation should be concentrated intentionally around:

- elemental identity;
- magic;
- living systems;
- focal landmarks;
- cultural ornament;
- narrative emphasis.

Not every object should compete at maximum saturation.

Each location owns a local palette. There is no universal global palette.

---

## 2.5 Light Is Part of Level Design

Lighting is compositional structure, not polish added at the end.

A scene should establish:

- a dominant light idea;
- a clear focal value range;
- readable shadow families;
- depth between foreground, midground, and background;
- intentional contrast around gameplay information.

A useful recurring pattern is:

```text
quieter / cooler framing
→ readable traversal space
→ strongest focal lighting at destination or threat
```

This is a grammar, not a mandatory color scheme.

---

## 2.6 Detail Has Hierarchy

Detail density must vary across the image.

### Low-detail zones
- distant background;
- framing masses;
- traversal surfaces away from focal events;
- broad terrain.

### Medium-detail zones
- nearby architecture;
- active environment systems;
- secondary props;
- traversable landmarks.

### High-detail zones
- characters;
- story objects;
- interactables;
- boss arenas;
- focal architecture;
- important machines;
- close-up hero assets.

Empty space is an artistic tool.

---

## 2.7 Materials Read Before Textures

A player should recognize stone, wood, cloth, metal, ice, flesh, glass, water, bark, leather, and magical surfaces from broad response before inspecting texture detail.

Prioritize:

- color family;
- roughness;
- reflectivity;
- translucency;
- edge behavior;
- wear logic;
- moisture / dirt / age placement.

Avoid high-frequency texture noise as a substitute for material identity.

---

## 2.8 Architecture and Nature Must Speak Differently

Built forms express intention:

- culture;
- technology;
- religion;
- engineering;
- status;
- historical period.

Natural forms express process:

- growth;
- erosion;
- pressure;
- weather;
- flow;
- decay;
- elemental influence.

Their collision tells environmental stories.

An overgrown ruin should not merely contain plants. Nature should visibly invade, bend, split, stain, bury, reclaim, or redirect construction.

---

## 2.9 Monumentality Is a Recurring Emotional Tool

The world should regularly contain things larger than Grace:

- geology;
- architecture;
- ancient machinery;
- organisms;
- celestial phenomena;
- magical structures;
- industrial systems.

Scale should communicate wonder, history, danger, or divine ambition.

Monumental does not mean uniformly gigantic. Small spaces become more powerful when contrasted with large ones.

---

## 2.10 Atmosphere Supports Depth, Never Clutter

Fog, dust, spores, rain, snow, smoke, pollen, embers, magical motes, steam, and similar effects should clarify:

- depth;
- air volume;
- motion;
- climate;
- elemental behavior;
- mood.

They should not become a permanent layer of screen noise.

Atmosphere is added after composition succeeds without it.

---

## 2.11 Characters Stay Cleaner Than Their Environments

Grace and important characters should maintain:

- strong silhouettes;
- controlled material complexity;
- readable body motion;
- intentional contrast against the environment;
- recognizable color / shape identity at gameplay distance.

The environment may be materially rich, but characters should not disappear into equivalent texture density.

---

## 2.12 Every Location Needs a Visual Thesis

A location is never just:

- forest;
- desert;
- laboratory;
- factory;
- town;
- temple;
- dreamscape.

Its brief should define a relationship between:

- shape;
- palette;
- material;
- lighting;
- culture;
- history;
- elemental theme;
- gameplay function.

A good one-sentence location thesis should imply what the player will see before individual assets are listed.

# 3. Global Composition Grammar

## The Two-Second Read

On first viewing, a gameplay shot should communicate its primary hierarchy within roughly two seconds.

A healthy hierarchy commonly resembles:

```text
PLAYER / THREAT / INTERACTION
          ↓
TRAVERSAL OR ACTION SPACE
          ↓
DESTINATION / LANDMARK / SECONDARY READ
          ↓
ATMOSPHERIC SUPPORT
```

Not every scene is centered or symmetrical. The Green shrine happens to use symmetry because sacred destination architecture benefits from it.

The global rule is **intentional hierarchy**, not symmetry.

---

# 4. Shape Language

Global shape language should favor:

- strong silhouettes;
- broad readable masses;
- deliberate exaggeration;
- controlled curvature;
- clear scale relationships;
- fewer better forms over many interchangeable forms.

Location dialects may push toward:

- angular;
- soft;
- vertical;
- sprawling;
- mechanical;
- organic;
- radial;
- fragmented;
- monumental;
- intimate.

The global consistency comes from readable form hierarchy, not from every biome sharing the same shapes.

---

# 5. Color Language

Global color principles:

- use richer saturation than strict realism;
- preserve believable local light relationships;
- keep neutrals available so saturated accents have power;
- assign color roles within each scene;
- use value and hue contrast to protect gameplay readability;
- allow elemental magic to exceed normal environmental saturation intentionally.

Every location should identify:

```text
dominant family
supporting family
shadow family
focal warm/cool contrast
rare accent color(s)
```

These values change by location.

---

# 6. Lighting Language

Global lighting should feel authored rather than uniformly exposed.

Favor:

- strong key-light ideas;
- readable local contrast;
- believable bounce / ambient families;
- atmospheric perspective;
- controlled specular response;
- selective darkness;
- focal pools of light.

Avoid:

- lighting every surface equally;
- bloom as a substitute for composition;
- excessive colored lights with no narrative source;
- flattening the environment to preserve detail everywhere.

Darkness may hide detail. That is allowed.

---

# 7. Surface and Material Density

The game should sit between painterly simplicity and physically convincing material richness.

### Large surfaces
Use broad tonal variation and readable material response.

### Medium breakup
Use cracks, joints, erosion, seams, grain, growth, staining, and construction logic.

### Fine breakup
Reserve for focal assets and close gameplay distance.

Do not distribute procedural noise uniformly across every surface.

---

# 8. Motion as Art Direction

Motion contributes to the visual identity.

Environmental motion should be selective:

- leaves react to wind;
- cloth follows movement and airflow;
- water communicates direction and force;
- smoke and particles inherit local conditions;
- machinery has readable operating rhythms;
- magical effects move according to elemental identity.

Nothing should move merely because movement is available.

Stillness is also a visual tool.

---

# 9. VFX Language

VFX should prioritize shape and timing over particle count.

Strong VFX usually have:

- one readable primary shape;
- a clear anticipation / impact / decay rhythm;
- color hierarchy;
- controlled secondary particles;
- strong interaction with light and environment;
- elemental specificity.

Avoid permanent visual fog around the player or environment.

---

# 10. Character–Environment Relationship

Grace is the visual anchor through wildly different locations.

Global character presentation should preserve:

- a readable head / torso / limb silhouette;
- controlled costume complexity;
- material separation at gameplay distance;
- expressive animation readable without close-up cinematics;
- environmental rim, bounce, or value separation when necessary.

Characters may inherit local lighting and weather, but should not become unreadable simply because a biome is visually dense.

---

# 11. Art-Production Workflow

## Phase 1 — Blockout in Godot

Use simple placeholders only:

- boxes;
- planes;
- cylinders;
- ramps;
- primitive collision;
- simple proxy meshes.

Godot answers spatial questions, not final modeling questions.

Judge:

- composition;
- scale;
- traversal;
- camera;
- sightlines;
- encounter space;
- landmark placement.

## Phase 2 — Concept Against the Exact Blockout

Generate or paint concept art using the existing gameplay composition as reference.

Do not redesign the whole room while trying to decide materials or style.

## Phase 3 — Extract Visual Rules

From successful concepts, identify:

- shape language;
- palette roles;
- material families;
- detail density;
- lighting hierarchy;
- vegetation / dressing logic;
- focal asset requirements.

Promote only genuinely global rules into this document.

## Phase 4 — Build a Tiny Modular Asset Kit

Before producing a whole biome, create a minimal kit of representative assets in a dedicated modeling tool such as Blender.

Typical first kit:

- one major terrain/cliff module;
- two or three rock variants;
- one traversal surface family;
- one architectural column / support family;
- one beam / trim family;
- one roof / cap family;
- two vegetation families;
- one focal prop.

## Phase 5 — Import and Test in the Blockout

Replace placeholders selectively.

Ask whether the kit actually produces the intended image in motion.

If not, revise the style before multiplying assets.

## Phase 6 — Expand the Kit

Only after the small kit succeeds should asset production scale outward.

## Phase 7 — Atmosphere and VFX Last

Particles, fog dressing, decals, microprops, clutter, and additional surface breakup are final support layers.

# 12. Art-Direction Guardrails

The following are explicit anti-patterns discovered during the Green Grotto study.

## Do not use procedural density as art direction

Procedural tools are excellent at variation and population after a visual language exists. They should not decide the composition by themselves.

## Do not decorate a confused blockout

If the space cannot be understood, remove or reshape forms first.

## Do not change unrelated systems during an art study

Environment art-direction work should not redesign gameplay HUD, combat systems, menus, or unrelated interfaces unless those are specifically under study.

## Do not equate realism with noise

More cracks, pebbles, moss, particles, and texture frequency do not automatically produce greater realism.

## Do not distribute detail uniformly

Focal hierarchy requires quiet areas.

## Do not force one successful biome onto the whole game

A successful local palette, architectural motif, or material combination remains local unless it proves useful across strongly different environments.

# 13. Green Earth Shrine — What Graduates to Global

The Green shrine study successfully demonstrates several global principles:

### Global
- clear player → route → destination composition;
- broad forms before detail;
- stylized geometry with grounded material response;
- cool / warm focal separation;
- wet surfaces used to reinforce lighting and depth;
- detail concentrated around sacred architecture;
- restrained vegetation framing traversal;
- monumental canyon framing;
- character silhouette kept cleaner than the environment.

### Green-Earth-specific
- moss / jade vegetation;
- teal water;
- wet canyon stone;
- East Asian-inspired shrine roof language;
- amber lanterns;
- sacred circular emblem;
- lotus / lily-pad accents;
- overgrown prehistoric sanctuary mood.

Those local traits should become a future **Green Earth dialect document**, not global defaults.

---

# 14. Cross-Biome Consistency Test

A proposed global rule should survive wildly different test cases.

Ask whether it still improves:

- Fire logging operations in Madagascar;
- Earth prehistoric ruins in China;
- Water shipwreck coves in Greece;
- Poison volcanic Iceland;
- Soul on Everest;
- Dreams in surreal liminal spaces;
- Lightning industrial Britain;
- Death in the Niagara corpse-brain laboratory;
- Body in a haunted Western town;
- Time in a Renaissance Italian workshop;
- Space across abandoned alien planets.

If a rule only helps one of these, it probably belongs in a lower-level dialect.

---

# 15. Global Art Review Checklist

Before approving a scene or concept, ask:

### Composition
- Is the primary read obvious?
- Is traversal understandable?
- Does the eye know where to go?

### Form
- Does the scene work without surface detail?
- Are major silhouettes distinct?

### Color
- Is the palette saturated with intention rather than everywhere?
- Are focal accents protected by quieter colors?

### Lighting
- Is there a dominant lighting idea?
- Does lighting clarify hierarchy and depth?

### Materials
- Do surfaces read from broad response before texture noise?
- Is weathering placed logically?

### Detail
- Is detail concentrated where it matters?
- Are there quiet areas?

### Character readability
- Does Grace remain readable against the scene?

### Atmosphere
- Does it create depth without obscuring gameplay?

### Identity
- Does the location have a distinct local dialect?
- Does it still obey the global visual grammar?

---

# Status

**v0.1 establishes the global grammar.**

The next art-direction work should test this bible against multiple radically different locations before any rule is treated as permanent.

Green Earth is the first proof case. The next strong validation target should intentionally differ in palette, architecture, technology, and mood so the global rules are forced to prove themselves.
